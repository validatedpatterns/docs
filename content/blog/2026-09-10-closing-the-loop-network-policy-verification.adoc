== Intent is not verification

A Kubernetes NetworkPolicy declares intent: _this pod should only accept connections from the ingress namespace through port 8443._ But you know the line about good intentions, right? Writing a policy and applying it to a cluster is only half the job. You also need to verify that what you intended is what happens in reality.

The https://validatedpatterns.io/blog/2026-06-29-zero-trust-network-policies/[first article] in the Layered Zero Trust Validated Pattern series showed how this pattern combines default-deny policies with per-pod allow rules to limit the damage potential of a compromised workload in accordance with https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final[NIST SP 800-207].

The https://validatedpatterns.io/blog/2026-07-29-zero-trust-runtime-threat-response/[second article] showed how Red Hat Advanced Cluster Security enforces those boundaries at runtime.

The third article in the series pulls network policy intent into the real world.

* Learn from seven common mistakes that undermine network policies.
* Understand the limitations of the policy analysis tooling landscape.
* See how the Layered Zero Trust Validated Pattern uses a design-test-deploy-observe loop to close the gap between policy intent and real-world impact.
